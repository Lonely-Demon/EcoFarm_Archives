# Price, market, and weather-linked decision evidence

## TNAU groundnut price forecast
Source: Tamil Nadu Agricultural University, Price Forecast for Groundnut, 2024, https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/tnaupressnotes/pdf/2024/Price%20Forecast%20for%20G.%20nut.pdf; access date 2026-08-13; primary institutional source.

The note says the TN-IAM/CARDS price-forecasting scheme analyzed 16 years of historical prices at Tindivanam Regulated Market and surveyed the market. It identifies Namakkal among major Tamil Nadu groundnut-growing districts. For the specific forecast, it estimated a farmgate price of Rs 80–85/kg for good-quality groundnut at harvest, while warning that price could change with market arrivals and monsoon; it advised farmers to consider the sowing decision accordingly. This is a dated, location-specific forecast from TNAU, not a Namakkal realized price or guarantee. EcoFarm should not reproduce the number without timestamp, market basis, quality assumptions, and uncertainty.

## Tamil Nadu e-NAM
Source: Department of Agricultural Marketing and Agri Business, Government of Tamil Nadu, e-NAM scheme page, https://www.agrimark.tn.gov.in/index.php/scheme/view/4; access date 2026-08-13; primary official.

The page states that e-NAM supports electronic trading, bidding, auctioning, assaying, market access, crop-advisory and market-price information through SMS to registered farmers. It reports 23 of Tamil Nadu’s 282 regulated markets integrated under e-NAM at the time of the page’s publication and lists Paramathivelur among Phase II integrated markets. It reports 26.95 lakh quintals transacted through the 23 markets, valued at Rs 484.12 crore, and 1,235 unified single licenses issued. These figures are page-reported and date-sensitive. The existence of an information/market channel is verified; its usability for Namakkal groundnut smallholders is unknown.

## Scientific Reports groundnut study
Source: Muthuswamy et al. (2025), “Weather-driven groundnut price forecasting and profitability assessment of cropping patterns in Tamil Nadu using boosting algorithms,” Scientific Reports 15, 33880, DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-08573-3; full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12484663/; accessed 2026-08-13.

The study surveyed 180 farmers across Erode, Namakkal, and Salem: two blocks per district, two villages per block, and 15 farmers per village. Namakkal sites listed were Tiruchengode/Vattur, Tiruchengode/Molasi, Rasipuram/Pattanam, and Rasipuram/Singalanthapuram. It combined farmer interviews on input costs, practices, yield, and diversification with weekly groundnut prices from AGMARKNET and weather data for 2010–2023. The paper reports that weather-linked multivariate models improved price-forecast accuracy and that a Namakkal onion-involving cropping pattern had the highest reported benefit-cost ratio in its analysis (2.18). The paper is evidence that crop choice and market timing can be analyzed, not evidence that a farmer-facing prediction product is safe or valued. Its purposive district/block/village selection limits transferability.
