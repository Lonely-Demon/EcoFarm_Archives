# EcoFarm Field Interview Guide — Revised

**Purpose.** Reconstruct a specific farmer decision, identify who actually makes and executes it, document existing advice and alternatives, and follow the event through action and outcome. This is a research instrument, not an app-pitch script. Ask about the most recent concrete event before asking about any hypothetical service.

## Interview opening and consent

Explain: “We are studying how farm decisions are made. We are not selling inputs, giving official advice, guaranteeing a price, yield, rainfall, or profit, or asking you to change a farm practice today. There are no right answers.” Ask permission to take notes and separately ask permission for a follow-up visit or call. Ask before viewing or photographing any receipt, field note, message, seed packet, weather bulletin, or phone screen. Do not collect unnecessary names, identity numbers, or financial details.

Assign a participant and event code. Record date, village, crop, approximate holding band, water situation, interviewer, recruitment route, language, and whether the participant is independent of the founder’s family or social network.

## A. Farm and recent crop-cycle context

1. Tell me about the most recent crop you completed or are currently managing. What was planted, when, on how much land, and with what water source?
2. Which other crops did you consider or grow in the last two seasons? What made you choose or reject them?
3. What changed in land, water, labour, seed, cash, credit, or market access over the last three seasons?
4. Which decision from that crop cycle was most difficult? Please describe one specific occasion rather than a general problem.

## B. Household decision-authority module

For the specific decision under discussion, ask each question neutrally and record a role code rather than assuming the interviewee is the sole decision-maker.

5. Who first noticed or raised the issue? **Initiator.**
6. Who made the final decision, and who could have said no? **Primary decision-maker and veto holder.**
7. Who influenced the decision, and what did each person say? **Influencers/advisers.**
8. Who paid, borrowed, or authorized the spending? **Payer/funder.**
9. Who physically carried out or arranged the work? **Executor.**
10. Who owns or controls the phone, SIM, or message account used for farm information? **Phone owner.**
11. Who reads, translates, or interprets a call, SMS, WhatsApp message, bulletin, or video? **Interpreter.**
12. Who should we contact to confirm what actually happened after the decision? **Follow-up authority.**
13. Was any person excluded from the conversation even though they had to pay, veto, interpret, or execute? What happened?

Where appropriate and consented, conduct a short follow-up with another relevant household member or worker. Record whether each authority fact is self-reported, observed, document-supported, third-party corroborated, or unknown.

## C. Candidate A: sow / wait / re-sow / early-establishment event

14. Think about the last time you decided whether to sow, wait, re-sow, reduce the area, or adjust the crop plan. What was the exact trigger?
15. When did you first notice or receive the trigger? How much time did you believe you had to decide?
16. Before speaking with anyone from this study, what were you planning to do next if nobody contacted you? Why?
17. What other options did you consider, including doing nothing? What made each option possible or impossible?
18. Which information did you check before deciding? Ask separately about observed rainfall, soil moisture, forecast, seed, labour, input cost, water, household cash, and market expectation.
19. Who did you consult, in what order, and at what time? What did each source actually say?
20. What action did you take, and when? Record sow, wait, re-sow, adjust, switch, seek advice, or non-action without judging it.
21. What happened afterward? Ask about emergence, affected acreage, repeat action, seed/labour/input cost, and crop stage.
22. If the decision was not recent, what artifact or person could help confirm the date, cost, advice, or action?

## D. Candidate B: in-season crop-risk response

23. Tell me about the most recent pest, disease, weed, nutrient, or moisture concern. What did you see, where, and when?
24. Who first noticed it and who decided what to do?
25. Before any study contact, what would you have done next if nobody from the study had spoken with you?
26. Who identified or discussed the problem? Did anyone inspect the field or ask for an image? What did each source recommend?
27. What options were considered: inspect, monitor, seek qualified review, treat, repeat treatment, or take no action?
28. What action actually happened, when, who paid, and who executed it?
29. What happened afterward? Record symptom progression, repeat action, cost, crop response, and whether the cause remained uncertain.
30. If a chemical or disease-treatment question arises, do not ask the farmer to endorse a product. Record the existing advice and refer the question to the qualified human review process.

## E. Harvest, storage, and sale event

31. Tell me about the most recent sale or harvest decision. What was the date, crop, quantity, quality basis, buyer/market route, transport, deductions, and final price if known?
32. What would you have done if nobody from the study contacted you? What made that baseline plan likely?
33. Which alternatives did you consider, and why were they accepted or rejected? Ask about trader, regulated market, e-NAM-linked route, FPO, processor, storage, transport, household cash, and debt without implying that delay is better.
34. What information did you check, when, and from whom? What differed between the observed quote and final transaction?
35. What happened after the sale or after any decision to delay? Record receipt or other artifact only with consent.

## F. Existing sources and trust

36. When advice conflicts, tell me about the last time it happened. Who gave each view, and which one won?
37. What makes a source trustworthy or untrustworthy? Ask about previous accuracy, locality, timing, explanation, accountability, language, ability to inspect, and commercial interest.
38. Tell me about a time you ignored advice. What happened afterward?
39. Tell me about a time you tried new advice. Did you repeat it? Why or why not?
40. Which sources are available but not used? Why: unavailable, unknown, too far, poor service, low relevance, low trust, or another reason?
41. What phone or non-phone channels do you actually use for farm information? Ask about calls, voice, WhatsApp, SMS, video, TV, paper, meetings, dealer contact, and assisted use without assuming a smartphone.

## G. Baseline, exposure, and attribution module

Complete before showing or describing an EcoFarm workflow.

42. On a 0–10 scale, how confident were you in the baseline plan?
43. What could have changed your plan before the decision window closed?
44. What other advice, weather event, household discussion, cash change, labour change, or buyer interaction might change it?

If a research workflow is shown later, record the exact output, source, timestamp, language, channel, and who interpreted it. Then ask:

45. What, if anything, changed in your plan after seeing or hearing this?
46. Which part mattered, if any? What other source or constraint also mattered?
47. What action did you actually take, and when?
48. What would you have done if the study information had disagreed with your trusted source?
49. On a 0–10 scale, how confident are you now? This is a perception measure, not proof of impact.

Do not ask “Did EcoFarm help?” until the neutral reconstruction is complete. If asked at the end, code the answer as self-attributed perception, not causal evidence.

## H. Artifact and data-quality module

50. Is there a receipt, field note, seed packet, input bill, sale record, weather bulletin, message, photograph, or person who can confirm any part of the timeline?
51. May we record that artifact or ask the person for a short confirmation? What information should remain private?
52. Which facts are estimates: acreage, cost, date, quantity, yield, price, or timing? What makes the estimate uncertain?

For every field, code: **observed; document-supported; third-party corroborated; self-reported; or unknown**. Never silently correct contradictory answers. Record the source, date, and relationship.

## I. Follow-up

53. What is the next observable step and by when should we contact you?
54. Which household member or worker should be included in the follow-up?
55. May we contact you after the decision window? What is the safest and most convenient channel?
56. At follow-up, what actually happened, who acted, what did it cost, and what outcome was observed?
57. What other advice or event occurred between the first interview and the action?

## J. Closing

58. Of the decisions discussed, which one recurs most often and has the greatest consequence?
59. Which decision is genuinely difficult because information is missing, late, conflicting, or hard to act on, rather than because of water, labour, cash, storage, or buyer constraints?
60. What would make a future research follow-up useful or burdensome?

Only after all past-behavior questions are complete, if appropriate: “If someone showed you a short, local-language comparison of a few feasible options with the source and uncertainty explained, where in your existing process could that fit?” Do not ask for willingness to use, pay, or download an app as a primary research measure.

## Interview coding

Code each event as: candidate; trigger; event state; crop; water; initiator; decision-maker; influencer; payer; veto holder; executor; phone owner; interpreter; source; baseline action; EcoFarm exposure; alternative explanation; time window; actual action; outcome; evidence class; trust reason; follow-up status; attribution status; and safety escalation. Separate **interest, understanding, declared intention, baseline plan, changed plan, trial, repeat use, follow-up confirmed action, and verified outcome**.
