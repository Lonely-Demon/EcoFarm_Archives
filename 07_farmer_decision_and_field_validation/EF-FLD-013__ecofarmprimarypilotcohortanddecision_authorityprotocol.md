# EcoFarm Primary Pilot Cohort and Decision-Authority Protocol

## 1. Sampling principle

The field programme must separate **discovery**, **primary monitored**, **comparison**, and **adviser/market** participants. A broad interview pool is not a monitored cohort. The primary monitored cohort is the minimum group from which EcoFarm may assess whether a decision event is recurrent, observable, actionable, and measurable.

The sample is purposeful, not statistically representative. Reports must never generalize it to Namakkal or Tamil Nadu without a separate sampling design.

## 2. Minimum cohort structure

| Cohort | Minimum target | Purpose | Evidence status permitted |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Discovery farmers | 12 total, including at least 8 target-segment farmers | Reconstruct past decisions, vocabulary, current workarounds, and household authority | Recalled events and planned events; discovery only. |
| Primary monitored cohort | **12 farmers minimum**, of whom at least 8 must fit the provisional target segment and at least 6 must be independent of founder/family networks | Follow real events and record baseline, EcoFarm exposure if any, action, follow-up, and outcome | Live workflow evidence; wedge selection requires minimum thresholds in the tie-break protocol. |
| Comparison-crop cohort | 8 farmers minimum, at least 4 in a non-groundnut crop system and at least 2 with different water access from the primary cohort | Test whether groundnut is the strongest decision wedge or merely the easiest access route | Comparative recurrence, consequence, actionability, and outcome evidence. |
| Adviser/market interviews | 6 minimum: at least 2 KVK/Department or agronomy sources, 2 input dealers/field workers, and 2 trader/regulated-market/FPO actors where available | Map existing advice, timing, conflict, incentives, and referral feasibility | Alternative and trust evidence; not farmer-demand proof. |
| Household authority follow-ups | All 12 primary monitored farmers; at least one additional authority/influencer for 8 of them | Verify who initiates, influences, funds, vetoes, receives/interprets information, and executes | Authority and attribution evidence. |

**Minimum target-segment cohort before a wedge conclusion:** 8 primary monitored farmers fitting the target definition, with at least 6 independent of founder/family networks, and at least 6 events reaching follow-up-confirmed action status. If these conditions are not met, the result is **not selected / insufficient evidence**.

## 3. Target-segment definition

A target-segment farmer should meet at least four of the following five conditions: the holding is approximately up to 2 hectares; groundnut or a comparable dryland crop was grown in the last two seasons; water is rainfed or materially uncertain; the participant or household makes the relevant decision; and the decision can be followed up within the programme period. Record exact facts where available; do not force a farmer into the segment based on a label.

A farmer may be included in the comparison cohort if they grow maize, sorghum, pulses, tapioca, vegetables, or another locally material crop system, or if they have dependable irrigation, different market access, or materially different advisory access. The comparison is designed to test decision recurrence and addressability, not to compare crop profitability.

## 4. Independence and recruitment controls

At least half of the primary monitored cohort must be recruited through a route independent of the founder’s family and immediate social network. Record recruitment route for every participant. Do not count a participant as independent merely because the interview occurred away from the founder’s home. A village facilitator, KVK contact, FPO, local institution, or unrelated referral may be used, provided the relationship and possible selection bias are recorded.

Avoid recruiting only farmers who are unusually articulate, digitally comfortable, already favorable to EcoFarm, or dependent on one adviser. Record refusals and basic reason where volunteered; a refusal log helps identify trust and access bias.

## 5. Household decision-authority module

For each candidate event, complete the following matrix. Do not assume the interviewee is the owner of the decision.

| Role | Person/code | What authority means | How to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initiator |  | First notices or raises the decision | Ask for the most recent event and who first raised it. |
| Primary decision-maker |  | Makes the final choice | Ask who could have said no and whose decision was followed. |
| Influencer/adviser |  | Changes the decision without owning it | Record each source separately and timing of advice. |
| Payer/funder |  | Controls cash, credit, or input purchase | Ask who paid, borrowed, or authorized spending. |
| Veto holder |  | Can prevent action because of land, water, labour, debt, or household priorities | Ask what would have stopped the action. |
| Executor |  | Carries out sowing, inspection, treatment, harvest, or sale | Ask who physically performed or arranged the action. |
| Phone owner |  | Controls the device or SIM receiving information | Observe/ask with consent; do not infer from household ownership. |
| Interpreter |  | Reads, translates, or explains messages | Ask who interprets a call, SMS, WhatsApp, or bulletin. |
| Follow-up authority |  | Can report the actual outcome after the event | Confirm the person and safe channel. |

For every role, record whether the fact is self-reported, observed, document-supported, or corroborated by another household member. Include women, younger members, hired workers, and other relevant participants where they materially influence the decision, without assuming that the senior male interviewee is the sole authority.

## 6. Recruitment and visit sequence

First conduct an individual discovery interview with the person who appears to own the decision. Then ask whether another household member or worker should be included in a short authority follow-up. For primary monitored participants, obtain consent for event notifications and a follow-up after the decision window. Never require phone access as a condition of participation.

Use assisted calls, voice messages, paper diaries, or facilitator visits where appropriate. Record whether the participant received information directly, through another person, or not at all. An action made after a message interpreted by another household member is a household-level event, not necessarily an individual digital-adoption event.

## 7. Cohort inclusion and exclusion

Include farmers who can identify a recent specific decision, allow a safe follow-up route, and fall within the stated crop/water/authority quotas. Exclude or defer participants where consent is unclear, a broker or adviser answers all questions, the participant is pressured to join, a commercial relationship creates an undisclosed conflict, or the decision cannot be followed up within the study period.

Do not exclude low-literacy, non-smartphone, or non-digital participants. Their inclusion is necessary to test assisted and mediated use. Do not include a farmer in a product-performance denominator if the farmer never encountered a relevant event or if the team cannot establish what action occurred.

## 8. Cohort-level decision rules

The cohort can support a narrow wedge recommendation only if the same decision appears independently across the primary target cohort, at least one comparison group has been examined, a sufficient number of events reach follow-up, and the household authority and baseline action are recorded. If the event is recurrent but the binding constraint is credit, water, labour, storage, or buyer power rather than information, classify the problem as **real but not software-addressable** and defer the build.
