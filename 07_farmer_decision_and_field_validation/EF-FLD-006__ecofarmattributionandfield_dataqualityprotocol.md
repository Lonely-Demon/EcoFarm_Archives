# EcoFarm Attribution and Field-Data Quality Protocol

## 1. Purpose

A farmer taking an action after speaking with the EcoFarm team does not prove that EcoFarm caused the action or improved the outcome. This protocol records what would have happened without EcoFarm, what other advice and constraints were present, and what evidence supports each field.

## 2. Required event record

Every monitored event receives a unique event ID and the following record.

| Field | Required capture |
|---|---|
| Participant and household code | Use codes; do not place names in the analysis file. |
| Candidate event | Sowing/early-establishment or crop-risk response. |
| Trigger time | When the farmer first noticed or received the trigger. |
| Baseline action | Before EcoFarm exposure, ask: “If nobody from this study contacted you, what would you do next, and by when?” Record exact answer, not an interpretation. |
| Baseline confidence | 0–10 confidence before any EcoFarm input. |
| Existing sources | Every advice source, message, visit, dealer/trader interaction, family discussion, and official bulletin already received. Record time and content as recalled or documented. |
| EcoFarm exposure | What was shown/said, by whom, when, and in what channel. Preserve the exact output or a transcript. |
| Alternative options | What the farmer could realistically have done, including no action. |
| Farmer response | Accepted, modified, rejected, deferred, or did not receive/understand. Record the farmer’s reason. |
| Action time | When the action was executed. |
| Constraints | Rainfall, water, seed, labour, cash, credit, transport, storage, buyer, household authority, or other. |
| Follow-up | Recontact after the action window; record actual action, outcome, and new advice. |
| Attribution status | Attributed, partially attributed, attribution unresolved, or no EcoFarm exposure. |

## 3. Counterfactual interview sequence

Ask the baseline counterfactual **before** showing or describing an EcoFarm workflow. Use neutral language:

1. “What were you planning to do next if we did not speak today?”
2. “What made that your plan?”
3. “What other options did you consider?”
4. “Who else would you ask or wait for?”
5. “What could cause you to change that plan?”
6. “How confident are you in that plan from 0 to 10?”

After any EcoFarm exposure, ask:

1. “What, if anything, changed in your plan?”
2. “What part of the discussion mattered?”
3. “What other information or person influenced the final choice?”
4. “What would you have done if the EcoFarm information had disagreed with your trusted source?”
5. “What action did you actually take, and when?”

Never ask “Did EcoFarm help?” as the primary attribution question. It may be asked only after the neutral reconstruction and must be coded as a perception, not causal proof.

## 4. Attribution classification

| Classification | Minimum evidence | Permitted claim |
|---|---|---|
| No exposure | Farmer did not receive or understand EcoFarm input before action | No product contribution claim. |
| Attribution unresolved | Action followed exposure, but another source, constraint, or natural event could explain it | Report action only; do not claim EcoFarm caused it. |
| Partial contribution | Farmer explicitly identifies a bounded EcoFarm element, baseline and alternatives are recorded, and another influence also mattered | Report as self-attributed contribution with confounders. |
| Stronger attribution signal | Baseline action is recorded; EcoFarm changes the stated plan; timing is documented; other sources and constraints are recorded; action is follow-up confirmed; comparison events show the change is not universal | Report as a promising attribution signal, not causal proof. |
| Causal impact | Requires a separate, adequately designed comparative or experimental study | Not claimed in the 30–90 day field programme. |

## 5. Field-data quality hierarchy

Classify each field separately. An event can have a high-quality date but low-quality acreage or outcome.

| Evidence class | Definition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Observed | Team directly observed the action or a time-stamped live trigger | Strongest for action timing; still requires follow-up for outcome. |
| Document-supported | Receipt, field note, seed packet, weather bulletin, message, sale record, photograph metadata, or other artifact corroborates the field | Use for the corroborated fields only; check authenticity and date. |
| Third-party corroborated | Household member, worker, adviser, buyer, or other independent source confirms part of the event | Use cautiously; record relationship and possible incentive. |
| Self-reported | Farmer reports the fact without artifact or independent confirmation | Use for discovery and clearly labelled descriptive evidence; do not use alone for precise outcome claims. |
| Unknown | Not asked, unclear, contradictory, or unavailable | Exclude from denominators that require the field. |

## 6. Corroboration protocol

For each high-consequence event, seek at least two independent evidence types where feasible: farmer account plus field observation; farmer account plus receipt/message; farmer account plus household/worker confirmation; or farmer account plus adviser/market record. Do not pressure farmers to produce documents or share private messages. If corroboration is not feasible, mark the field self-reported or unknown rather than upgrading it by confidence of speech.

Resolve contradictions by preserving both values, recording the source and date, and assigning a reconciliation status. Do not silently average acreage, cost, or timing. If two sources disagree materially, report a range or classify the field unknown.

## 7. Outcome-claim discipline

A result may be included in an outcome denominator only when: the relevant event occurred; the action and timing are known; the farmer was recontacted after the decision window; the outcome is defined before analysis; and the field has observed, document-supported, or clearly labelled self-reported status. Exclude events from outcome claims when the participant withdrew consent, the action was never completed, the crop/event was not eligible, the timeline cannot be reconstructed, or the result is fully confounded by an unrecorded major event.

Do not claim yield, profit, price improvement, crop protection, or causal impact from a short field programme unless the required data and comparison design exist. Safe interim outcomes include: time to decision, number of advice sources checked, baseline versus post-exposure plan, follow-up completion, confidence change, escalation rate, and whether the farmer recorded an observable action.

## 8. Comparison and alternative explanations

For every apparent EcoFarm success, record at least one plausible alternative explanation: rainfall changed, another adviser intervened, the farmer had already decided, a household authority changed the plan, an input became available, a buyer changed terms, or the farmer selected the action independently. Compare monitored events with similar non-EcoFarm events where feasible, but do not call the comparison causal unless the design supports it.

## 9. Audit fields

The research lead must retain: original note or audio-consent record where applicable; exact EcoFarm output; timestamps; source links or bulletin date; event classification; authority matrix; baseline counterfactual; all advice sources; action; follow-up; corroboration status; and analyst decision. Analysts should not edit the original event record; corrections require a dated amendment.
